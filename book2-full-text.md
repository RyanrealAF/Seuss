### Chapter 1: Grid vs. Foot: Beat-Grid Mapping
#### 1.1 The Metronome and the Page
Imagine a producer in a digital audio workstation (DAW) placing 16th-note subdivisions on a grid. Scansion in Book 1 mapped feet (`x x /`). Book 2 translates speech feet onto a 16-subdivision musical grid.
#### 1.2 The 16-Subdivision Monospace Grid
A 4/4 measure consists of 4 main beats, each divided into 4 sixteenth notes (1-e-&-a, 2-e-&-a, 3-e-&-a, 4-e-&-a).
```
Subdivision: | 01 | 02 | 03 | 04 | 05 | 06 | 07 | 08 | 09 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 15 | 16 |
Beat:        | 1  |    | e  | a  | 2  |    | e  | a  | 3  |    | e  | a  | 4  |    | e  | a  |
Specimen:    | In | the| MORN|    | ing| I  | WALK|    | to | the| BRDG|    | by | the| SEA |    |
Meter:       | x  | x  | /  |    | x  | x  | /   |    | x  | x  | /  |    | x  | x  | /   |    |
```
#### 1.3 Grid-Level Rhythmic Coherence ($RC_{grid}$)
$$\text{Grid-Level Rhythmic Coherence } (RC_{grid}) = \left( \frac{\text{On-Grid Target Hits}}{\text{Total 16th Slots}} \right) \times 100\%$$

### Chapter 2: Anacrusis and the Pickup Beat
#### 2.1 The Run-Up Into the Bar
The run-up syllables (`x x`) in an anapest functioning as a musical pickup before the main beat are technically termed **anacrusis**.
#### 2.2 Anacrusis Across the Bar Line
Anacrusis crosses the measure boundary, pulling the listener into Beat 1 of the following bar.
#### 2.3 Theory: Anacrusis and Motor Readiness
Anacrusis builds auditory anticipation and motor readiness in the motor cortex.
#### 2.4 Worked Examples: 1-Syllable vs 2-Syllable Pickups
Comparing a 2-syllable anacrusis (*In the...*) with a 1-syllable anacrusis (*The...*).

#### 2.6 Common Mistakes

#### 2.7 Chapter Summary
Anacrusis is the technical term for unstressed pickup syllables preceding a primary downbeat.

### Chapter 3: Syncopation and the Displacement Engine
#### 3.1 Off-Beat Accentuation
Syncopation places stress where the pulse does not expect it, displacing the beat to off-beat subdivisions (e.g., the "e" or "a" of a beat).
#### 3.2 The Displacement Engine
Shifting a stress by a single 16th subdivision creates rhythmic drive without breaking the underlying tempo.
#### 3.3 Theory: Temporal Displacement and Prediction Error
Syncopation generates controlled prediction error, spiking listener attention.
#### 3.4 Worked Examples: On-Beat vs. Syncopated Displacement
Comparing a 16th-grid line with stresses on main beats versus off-beat 16th slots.
#### 3.5 Exercise: Syncopation Grid Drill
Displace 2 stresses onto off-beat subdivisions in a 16th grid table.
#### 3.6 Common Mistakes
Over-syncopating until the baseline beat-grid is lost entirely.
#### 3.7 Chapter Summary
Syncopation displaces stress onto weak subdivisions, driving rhythmic momentum.

### Chapter 4: Isochrony vs. Variable Delivery
#### 4.1 Equal Time vs. Dynamic Speech
**Isochrony** is the equal division of time across measures. Performers maintain equal measure duration while varying syllable density inside the measure.
#### 4.2 Speech-Rate Compression
Fitting 16 syllables into the same 4-beat duration as 12 syllables requires speech-rate compression.
#### 4.3 Theory: Perceived Isochrony in Speech
English speech is stress-timed; listeners perceive measures as equal in duration even when syllable counts vary.
#### 4.4 Worked Examples: Isochronous Bar Mapping
Showing two measures with different syllable counts occupying identical grid durations.
#### 4.5 Exercise: Compression Drill
Fit 14 syllables into a 16th grid measure without altering the metronome tempo.
#### 4.6 Common Mistakes
Slowing down the tempo when syllable count increases.
#### 4.7 Chapter Summary
Isochrony holds measure duration constant, allowing variable syllable density through compression.

### Chapter 5: The Accumulation Method (Additive Synthesis)
#### 5.1 Increasing Syllable Density
The **Accumulation Method** (also known by its alias *additive synthesis*, list as alias per Bible I-14) increases syllable count per bar across a verse to build urgency and climax.
#### 5.2 Density Trajectories
Moving from 12 syllables (Bar 1) $\rightarrow$ 14 syllables (Bar 2) $\rightarrow$ 16+ syllables (Bar 3), escalating semantic density.
#### 5.3 Theory: Information Density and Cognitive Acceleration
Increasing syllable rate increases information density, creating a feeling of emotional acceleration.
